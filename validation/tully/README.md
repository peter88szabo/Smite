# Tully's models: validating the coupling approximation

Smite derives nonadiabatic couplings from the curvature of the adiabatic energy
gap (Baeck-An), because a machine-learned surface offers no wavefunction to
differentiate. This directory answers the obvious question: **how good is that
approximation, and where does it break?**

Tully's three one-dimensional two-state models are the right place to ask.
Everything about them is analytic, including the exact coupling, so the
approximation can be compared with the truth point by point -- no trajectories,
no ambiguity about which part of the method is at fault.

J. C. Tully, *J. Chem. Phys.* **93**, 1061 (1990).

## Contents

| File | What |
| --- | --- |
| `tully_models.py` | the three models, their diabatic Hamiltonians and derivatives, adiabatic energies, the exact coupling in closed form, and Baeck-An's estimate |
| `compare_couplings.py` | runs the comparison, writes three `.dat` files and a figure |
| `test_tully_models.py` | 20 checks on the models and on what the approximation does |
| `Tully_SAC/`, `Tully_DAC/`, `Tully_ECR/` | `PESCalculator` interfaces, so the models can be run as ordinary Smite surfaces |
| `reference_exact_quantum.dat` | exact quantum branching for model I, from grid wavepacket propagation |

```bash
python compare_couplings.py
python -m pytest validation/tully -q
```

## The result

|  | at the peak of \|d12\| | over the coupling region |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **SAC** simple avoided crossing | 0.897 of exact | silent over 82% |
| **DAC** dual avoided crossing | 0.983 of exact | silent over 72% |
| **ECR** extended coupling | **1.732** of exact | never silent, and far too large |

Three things follow.

**At the crossing it is good.** Where the hop actually happens, Baeck-An is 10%
low on SAC and 2% low on DAC. For a system whose nonadiabatic behaviour is
concentrated at an avoided crossing, this is a sound approximation.

**It captures the peak and little else.** Away from the crossing the gap
curvature turns negative, and Baeck-An prescribes no coupling at all there. On
SAC it is silent across 82% of the region where the exact coupling is still
appreciable. Weak long-range coupling is simply not seen. That is conservative,
and for a localised crossing it costs little.

**On model III it fails, and not gracefully.** Extended coupling has no
localised avoided crossing -- `V12` tends to `2B` instead of decaying -- so the
premise of the method is absent. The approximation overestimates the peak by
73% and then pins at roughly 0.45 across the whole approach region while the
exact coupling decays:

| region | exact \|d12\| | Baeck-An | ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| x in [-6,-4) | 0.095 to 0.225 | 0.346 to 0.450 | 2.3 |
| x in [-4,-2) | 0.016 to 0.094 | 0.450 | 11.3 |
| x in [-2, 0) | 0.003 to 0.016 | 0.450 | **67.6** |
| x > 0 | decaying | **0** | 0 |

A spurious constant coupling two orders of magnitude too large, right where the
trajectory spends its approach, would drive heavy false hopping. Then nothing at
all past the crossing.

## What this means for a real system

A system behaving like SAC or DAC -- nonadiabaticity concentrated at an avoided
crossing, coupling decaying on both sides -- is within the method's competence,
with the caveat that weak tails are missed.

A system behaving like ECR is not, and the failure is silent: the couplings come
out plausible-looking and wrong. Before trusting curvature-derived couplings on
a new system, check that its adiabatic gap has a clear minimum with positive
curvature that decays on both sides. The `variance()` diagnostic on a
machine-learned surface answers a different question and will not catch this.

## The exact quantum reference

`reference_exact_quantum.dat` holds branching probabilities for model I from
grid wavepacket propagation on the same Hamiltonian, generated by the code in
`wavepacket/` (imported from this Dropbox library and corrected, see
`wavepacket/CHANGES.md`).

| k | P(upper), exact |
| --- | --- |
| 8.000 | 0.06104 |
| 10.000 | 0.15521 |
| 12.000 | 0.21876 |
| 15.000 | 0.32304 |
| 20.000 | 0.49301 |
| 30.000 | 0.71428 |
| 45.166 | 0.85871 |

All converged to five decimals. The step count is chosen per momentum so the
packet never reaches the periodic boundary; with a fixed count the fast packets
wrap around and re-enter from the far side. A previously saved value of 0.44392
at k=45.166 was corrupted that way -- 135 bohr of travel on an 80 bohr grid.

## The dynamics result

`run_fssh_dynamics.py` drives Smite's own integrator and FSSH propagator over an
ensemble and compares the fraction ending on the upper surface.
`plot_fssh_vs_exact.py` draws it. A crude screen at 100 trajectories per
momentum, so the error bars are around 0.035:

| k | FSSH | exact | difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8.00 | 0.000 +- 0.000 | 0.061 | -0.061 |
| 10.00 | 0.200 +- 0.040 | 0.155 | +0.045 |
| 12.00 | 0.180 +- 0.038 | 0.219 | -0.039 |
| 15.00 | 0.100 +- 0.030 | 0.323 | **-0.223** |
| 20.00 | 0.140 +- 0.035 | 0.493 | **-0.353** |
| 30.00 | 0.120 +- 0.033 | 0.714 | **-0.594** |
| 45.17 | 0.130 +- 0.034 | 0.859 | **-0.729** |

Up to k = 12 the agreement is within the sampling error. Above it the exact
result keeps rising while FSSH flattens at 0.12 to 0.14 and stays there. The
deviations at k >= 15 are seven to twenty standard errors, far outside what more
trajectories would change, so the shape of this conclusion does not depend on
the crude statistics.

The cause is the coupling, and it follows from the comparison above. A
trajectory accumulates transition probability as the integral of `v . d` through
the coupling region. The exact `d12` extends over roughly +-3 bohr; Baeck-An
reproduces its peak but collapses it to a spike about half a bohr wide. The
faster the trajectory, the less time it spends in that spike, so the hopping
falls away with increasing momentum. Under-hopping that worsens with k is what
the narrow spike predicts, and it is what the dynamics shows.

At low momentum the trajectory is slow enough that even the spike delivers
enough probability, which is why the first two points agree.

## Worth trying next

Widening the Baeck-An coupling, or restoring its tails rather than truncating
where the gap curvature turns negative, and seeing whether the exact curve comes
back. That is a change to the approximation itself, not to the hopping
machinery, which this comparison has now separated cleanly.
